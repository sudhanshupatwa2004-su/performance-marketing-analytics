# Executive Summary: WishCare-Inspired Performance Marketing Analysis

*Simulated data for a portfolio project. It does not show WishCare's actual campaign results.*
*Period: 1 Sep – 30 Oct 2026 (60 days) · 14 campaigns · Meta Ads, Google Ads, E-commerce marketplaces*

## Business question
Where is paid advertising making money, where is it losing money, and how should the budget move to grow
revenue and new customers more efficiently?

## Headline numbers (observed in the data)
| Ad Spend | Revenue | Orders | New Customers | ROAS | CAC | CTR | CPC | CPM | CVR | AOV |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ₹33.16 L | ₹72.67 L | 12,359 | 7,576 | 2.19x | ₹438 | 1.29% | ₹11.39 | ₹146.92 | 4.25% | ₹588 |

## Five things the data shows
1. **Google Ads is the most efficient channel.** It took 35% of spend but produced 50% of revenue (ROAS 3.13x, CAC ₹318).
   Meta Ads took the largest share of spend (44%) at ROAS 1.79x. E-commerce marketplaces converted visitors best
   (CVR 7.15%) but clicks cost the most (CPC ₹27.40), so ROAS was only 1.45x.
2. **Blended ROAS flatters performance.** Brand search alone produced 23% of revenue from 4% of spend (ROAS 12.41x),
   but only 29% of its buyers were new. Without brand search, ROAS drops from 2.19x to **1.76x**.
3. **Generic "hair fall" search is the best acquisition engine:** ROAS 3.15x, CAC ₹237, 84% new customers, 1,753 orders.
4. **One creative is wearing out.** The Vitamin C influencer video's CTR fell 24% between the first and last four
   weeks, and its ROAS fell 22%.
5. **The A/B test found a click winner, not a sales winner.** The UGC video got a 15.9% higher CTR than the static image
   (p < 0.001) and cheaper clicks, but conversion rate and ROAS were practically identical (p = 0.92 and 0.96).

## What it means (uses stated assumptions)
Assuming a 55% contribution margin on website sales and 40% on marketplace sales, break-even ROAS is 1.82x (website) and 2.50x (marketplace).
On that basis, **4 campaigns generate all the profit**, and **10 campaigns holding 78% of spend are below break-even**.
Overall contribution after ad cost is still positive (≈ ₹5.35 L).

## Recommended actions
| Action | Campaigns |
|---|---|
| **Scale test +25%** | GGL_Search_Generic_HairFall |
| **Maintain** (high ROAS, limited headroom) | GGL_Search_Brand, both Meta retargeting campaigns |
| **Optimise, hold budget** | Meta UGC hair serum, Google Shopping, Google PMax, Amazon Sponsored Products |
| **Reduce 30% and fix** | VitC influencer (refresh creative), Hair Serum static, Nykaa VitC, Flipkart shampoo |
| **Cap 15%, measure on reach** | Meta Reels awareness, YouTube awareness |

The plan keeps the budget the same. ₹5,963 a day is freed from cuts: ₹1,449 a day goes to the scale test and ₹4,514 a day funds new creative tests.
Excluding the unknown return from those tests, the scenario adds about **₹3,200 a day in contribution** (about ₹0.97 L per 30 days),
even though revenue falls by about ₹2,860 a day. In other words, it gives up loss-making revenue. *This is a planning scenario.
It needs a 2–4 week controlled budget test to confirm.*

## Limitations
Simulated data; last-click style attribution; margins and target CAC are assumptions; no customer lifetime value,
returns data or organic baseline; 60 days is too short to capture full seasonality.
